## Closest-work synthesis

RW1 and RW2 are the closest technical precedents. RW1 already formalizes operational ambiguity, authoritative evidence, postcondition verification, and terminal-belief correctness under controlled interface interventions. RW2 already supplies the non-atomic failure rationale and a verification-aware runtime mitigation. RW3 and RW4 further weaken any claim that postcondition-aware tooling itself is new. RW18 and RW19 strengthen the neighboring precedents for unreliable environments and state-based evaluation. RW20 demonstrates that tool presentation can itself change selection, so ProofPath's alias, order, paraphrase, and covariate controls are necessary rather than cosmetic. RW21--RW22 verify plans or commands before execution but impose an auditing mechanism rather than measuring voluntary demand for outcome evidence. RW23 reinforces the need to separate plan selection from execution fidelity, while RW24--RW25 broaden tool-use and tool-selection evaluation without introducing an outcome-evidence price curve.

The remaining defensible gap is narrower: **behavioral demand for verifiability before execution**, measured by choices among controlled plan alternatives and a verification-price curve. The current review did not identify a verified work that crosses this choice with failure probability and concrete consequence severity while evaluating ground-truth state.
